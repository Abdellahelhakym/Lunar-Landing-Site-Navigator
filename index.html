Limits

LOLA profiles are available only for sites and directions covered by the supplied DEM. Other sites use a clearly labeled flat-horizon approximation. The elevation unit in the GeoTIFF is unconfirmed. This terrain model does not validate the Sun/Earth ephemerides or certify mission feasibility.

About

Purpose

Lunar Landing Site Navigator compares candidate sites near the lunar south pole by how often the Sun (power) and Earth (direct communication) are above the local horizon, and how long periods without either last.

Project team: ABDELLAH EL HAKYM

Method

Sun and Earth azimuth and elevation were retrieved once from the NASA JPL Horizons API for each site (observer on the Moon, airless, UT time scale, 1-hour steps, 15 Nov 2026 to 15 Feb 2027) and stored as static files. Offline, the LOLA DEM is sampled along 360 azimuths with lunar curvature included. In the browser, a target is counted as visible when its elevation is above the horizon elevation at its azimuth; unavailable terrain profiles use a labeled flat horizon. Sun visibility, Earth visibility, darkness, communication outage and blackout are computed from hourly states. Score = wSun × Sun visible % + wEarth × Earth visible %, with adjustable weights (default 60/40).

Why the south pole is difficult

The Sun and Earth stay close to the horizon at many polar locations. Small changes in terrain, location, or arrival date can therefore change sunlight and direct-to-Earth visibility, while crater rims can block either one. Power and communications windows must be planned together; realistic estimates require local terrain data.

Assumptions

Longitude is East-positive. Elevations are airless (no refraction). Horizons ephemerides use site altitude 0 km; LOLA terrain profiles use the site pixel elevation as observer height. DEM elevations are treated as meters, but the GeoTIFF does not encode or independently confirm that unit. Sites are representative points, not official landing sites.

Terrain data

The supplied raster is from NASA Moon Trek, layer “LRO LOLA DEM 87.5 deg v4, S Pole”. The download date and exact selection box were not recorded. Profiles are generated offline from the supplied GeoTIFF using a 3,000 m search radius, 25 m sample spacing, 360 one-degree azimuths and lunar curvature. The observer is at the DEM pixel elevation; the Sun is treated as a point. Profiles are available for Shackleton and Artemis; Malapert, Haworth and Nobile are outside this subset. The vertical unit is assumed to be meters and remains unconfirmed. This is a geometric obstruction model, not continuous ray tracing or a certified power, communications, or landing-feasibility analysis.

Limitations

  • LOLA coverage is limited; unavailable sites use a flat 0° horizon, so Sun visibility is an upper bound.
  • Terrain horizons use sampled pixel centers within 3 km at 25 m spacing; this is not continuous ray tracing.
  • The LOLA GeoTIFF does not declare vertical units; confirm the DEM provenance before scientific or operational use.
  • The Sun is treated as a point, not a disk of about 0.5°.
  • “Visible” means above the horizon, not enough elevation for efficient solar power.
  • Hourly sampling can miss events shorter than one hour.
  • Periods that touch the start or end of the window are shown with “≥”.
  • Only a curated set of sites is available; adding one requires re-running the data script.

How to use

  1. Choose a site and dates in Analysis, then click Analyze.
  2. Move the time slider to see where the Sun and Earth are in the sky.
  3. Use Compare to rank several sites over the same period.
