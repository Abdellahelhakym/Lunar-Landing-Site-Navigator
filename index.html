Limits

A flat horizon gives an upper bound on Sun visibility. Terrain-based illumination requires a LOLA horizon profile (future work).

About

Purpose

Lunar Landing Site Navigator compares candidate sites near the lunar south pole by how often the Sun (power) and Earth (direct communication) are above the local horizon, and how long periods without either last.

Project team: ABDELLAH EL HAKYM

Method

Sun and Earth azimuth and elevation were retrieved once from the NASA JPL Horizons API for each site (observer on the Moon, airless, UT time scale, 1-hour steps, 15 Nov 2026 to 15 Feb 2027) and stored as static files. In the browser, a target is counted as visible when its elevation is above the horizon elevation at its azimuth. Sun visibility, Earth visibility, the longest darkness, the longest communication outage and the longest blackout (neither visible) are computed from these hourly states. Score = wSun × Sun visible % + wEarth × Earth visible %, with adjustable weights (default 60/40).

Assumptions

Longitude is East-positive. Elevations are airless (no refraction). Site altitude is set to 0 km relative to the ellipsoid. Sites are representative points, not official landing sites.

Limitations

  • No terrain: the horizon is flat (0°), so Sun visibility is an upper bound.
  • The Sun is treated as a point, not a disk of about 0.5°.
  • “Visible” means above the horizon, not enough elevation for efficient solar power.
  • Hourly sampling can miss events shorter than one hour.
  • Periods that touch the start or end of the window are shown with “≥”.
  • Only a curated set of sites is available; adding one requires re-running the data script.

Data and credits

Ephemerides: NASA JPL Horizons System (DE440/DE441). Reference literature: Gläser et al. (2018), “Illumination Conditions at the Lunar Poles: Implications for Future Exploration”, for the illumination context shown in Validation. Site context: IAU Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature.

How to use

  1. Choose a site and dates in Analysis, then click Analyze.
  2. Move the time slider to see where the Sun and Earth are in the sky.
  3. Use Compare to rank several sites over the same period.
